## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment (Assessment A):** Reads as crafted voice, not generic AI — but with three tell leakage points. The page earns its escape from the "AI made this" bucket through a committed, documented kicker-badge brand system and a belief-ladder strategy that drives content. It avoids the worst tells cleanly: no side-stripe accent borders (the only vertical stripe is the structural timeline `border-l-3 border-nb-border` in experiencesection.tsx:133, ink not accent), no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no hero-metric template (the hero uses a "Candidate Snapshot" field-list card, not three giant stat numbers — the single most important anti-slop decision, and it's correct), no numbered 01/02/03 scaffolding, no text overflow.

The leaks:
1. **The Services section is the canonical AI-portfolio 3-card grid** (servicessection.tsx:55 — three near-identical icon+title+description cards: Web/Mobile/Backend Development). This is the generic template, and it directly contradicts the core product principle "Show, don't tell — every claim about skill is backed by a real project or code link." Services is three unbacked claims. Highest slop risk on the page.
2. **Bare-eyebrow violations of the Kicker Badge Rule.** DESIGN.md §3 prohibits a lone tracked eyebrow above a heading, yet: herosection.tsx:157 (`Positioning`) floats bare inside the snapshot card; projectsection.tsx:104 (`project.type`) floats bare inside the project visual. These are exactly the "empty eyebrow" tell the system was built to avoid.
3. **Services + Skills + About-principles are three separate 3-up card grids** using the same nb-card silhouette — rhythmically repeating the same shape; the "identical card grid" tell at the section level.

**Deterministic scan (Assessment B):** `detect.mjs` over `app/page.tsx` and the full `app` + `components` tree returned EXIT 0 (clean) — 0 findings, no rule names, no file:line locations. No neobrutalist mislabels (offset shadows, border-radius, chroma) fired. The clean result confirms what the LLM review found: the design system is not leaking into detector-visible anti-patterns. No false positives to adjudicate.

**Visual overlays:** NOT produced. No browser automation / Playwright tool is available in this environment, so detect.js was not injected and no user-visible overlay exists. Browser evidence is a documented fallback; only the static CLI pass above is collected evidence.

**Synthesis:** The LLM review and the deterministic scan agree — the neobrutalist system is structurally clean and on-brand (no offset-shadow/border-radius/chroma false positives). The LLM caught the *semantic* slop the detector cannot: the Services 3-card template and two bare eyebrows. Detector clean + LLM flags = the issues are content/voice-level, not system-level. This is a genuinely strong base.

## Overall Impression

A confident, well-engineered neobrutalist portfolio that serves its recruiter audience well — the "Candidate Snapshot" / "What HRD can expect" block is excellent recruiter-aware craft. It sits in the recognizable 2025–2026 neobrutalism-on-cream lane, so it reads as "on-trend" rather than unmistakably personal. The single biggest opportunity is the **Services section**: it is both the clearest AI tell and a direct contradiction of the "show, don't tell" principle. Fixing it (plus the two bare eyebrows) moves the page from "good, on-trend" to "unmistakably Falih's."

## What's Working

1. **A genuinely executed design system** — tokens, hard zero-blur shadows, press transforms (globals.css:127-135), kicker badges, and the nb-heading-block highlight are applied consistently and documented. This is craft, not decoration; it embodies "the site itself is the strongest proof."
2. **Recruiter-optimized proof blocks** — the snapshot's "What HRD can expect" (herosection.tsx:174) and the skills section's level tags (skillsection.tsx:26-33) externalize exactly what a scanner needs in <2 min.
3. **Honest, outcome-led project case studies** — projectsection.tsx shows role, featured tagging, and concrete highlights; per-project role labels and "Featured collaboration" honesty serve the belief ladder.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Services section is generic fluff contradicting the strategy** — servicessection.tsx:7-29, three unbacked capability cards. Violates "Show, don't tell"; the page's weakest link and clearest AI tell; wastes the recruiter's scan budget. *Fix:* replace with an outcome/proof strip (3 "what you get" statements each linked to a project) or fold into Skills/Projects. *Command:* /impeccable distill (or clarify).

**[P1] Bare-eyebrow violations break the Kicker Badge Rule** — herosection.tsx:157 ("Positioning") and projectsection.tsx:104 (project.type) float as lone tracked eyebrows, exactly the empty-eyebrow tell DESIGN.md forbids. *Fix:* wrap in a bordered section-eyebrow badge or demote to plain text-nb-muted. For project.type, move into the bordered meta row. *Command:* /impeccable polish.

**[P2] Redundant claim restatement inflates cognitive load** — "React/Next.js/TS + AI/data" repeated across hero subtitle, snapshot Core stack/Focus, "What HRD can expect," and About. Repetition without differentiation doesn't add belief and slows the 2-min scan. *Fix:* make each block say something new (hero = who/availability, snapshot = proof pointers, about = story/working style, skills = stack map); cut duplicated phrasing. *Command:* /impeccable distill.

**[P2] Thin/unnamed work history is a trust valley** — experiencesection.tsx:17-50, no named employers; education org unnamed. Undercuts belief rung 1 ("real, serious person") for recruiters; highlights are generic verbs. *Fix:* name team/client where permissible; convert one highlight into a concrete outcome ("shipped X to Y users," "cut load time Z%"). *Command:* /impeccable harden (or clarify).

**[P3] Token/consistency drift vs DESIGN.md** — text-neutral-300/400 hardcoded in aboutsection.tsx:66 & footer.tsx:37-42; backend service card uses hover:shadow-nb (ink) while siblings use accent shadows (servicessection.tsx:28); hero aside mixes accent-2 chip + accent-3 bullets + accent top border (multi-accent in one block). *Fix:* swap neutral-* → text-white on dark; give backend card a consistent accent shadow; let hero aside be a deliberate exception or trim to one accent. *Command:* /impeccable audit (or colorize).

**Jordan (First-Timer, cold arrival):** Fine on desktop — name + role + snapshot orient instantly. Risk: surface-grid + 3 decorative shapes + two-column hero feel busy before reading. No fatal fail.

**Riley (Stress Tester):** Exercises menu, scroll-spy, form. Form validation solid. Riley will immediately spot Services as filler and the two bare eyebrows as rule breaks. No functional break, but credibility dents.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile):** Real fail — no persistent contact CTA on mobile. The header "Hire me" button (header.tsx:68-73) is `hidden md:flex`; on mobile the only persistent control is the hamburger. To contact, Casey must open the menu then tap Contact — two extra taps, and hero CTAs require scrolling. *Fix:* keep a compact "Hire me"/contact pill visible in the mobile header next to the hamburger.

**Rina (Recruiter/HRD scanner, project-specific):** Gets snapshot, skill levels, role-tagged projects, contact — good. But (a) Services wastes her scan; (b) unnamed employers in Experience lower trust; (c) if she bounces at Services she may never reach Projects/Contact. *Fix:* reorder so Projects sits higher, or compress Services into a one-line capability line above Skills.

## Questions to Consider

1. Does Services earn its screen real estate, or is it the one section that betrays "show, don't tell"? What if its column became a "proof of outcomes" strip linking each capability to a real project — would Rina believe faster?
2. Is seven sections too many for a 2-minute scan? Could About + Skills + Services collapse into a single "Capabilities" view that gets Rina to Projects + Contact two scrolls sooner?
3. The work history has no named employers — is that the single biggest trust gap, and would one concrete, named, quantified outcome out-perform all three generic bullet highlights combined?
4. Is the loud five-accent neobrutalist voice helping or hurting the "serious, recruiter-ready" belief with conservative HRD audiences? Would a calmer accent ratio (ember-only with teal as the sole counterweight) test as more credible without losing the brand?
